# Inspiration — "The Punch Line"

## The image

A joke and a rag are the same machine.

A joke works by building a grid of expectation and then landing somewhere
just off it — the pause, the swerve, the word that arrives one beat late and
means something else. That is syncopation, described exactly. The left
hand's oom-pah is the setup: four beats of law, stated over and over until
the ear signs the contract. The right hand is the comedian: it accepts the
grid precisely so it can land beside it. A tied note over the barline is a
deadpan held one beat too long. Stop-time is the pause before the punch
line. Ragtime is wit with a time signature — the first music whose whole
subject is *timing*.

**A rag is a joke told in eighth notes.**

## The paper

The physical setting: a punched pianola roll. Ragtime and the player piano
grew up together — rags were sold as paper for hands *and* paper for
machines, and the roll is the older sibling of the very MIDI file this
piece will be built in. Holes in paper, positions and durations, binary and
exact: the roll was digital music sixty years before digital.

And the roll houses the era's best inside joke. Arrangers at the roll
companies — QRS most famously — *doctored* their rolls: punched in extra
octaves, filler thirds, a fourth voice no ten fingers could reach, so the
parlor pianola sounded like a better pianist than any who had ever lived.
People bought a ghost with too many hands and loved it. The word "punch"
folds all of it together: the puncher's holes, the joke's landing, the
accent's snap.

## Why this one is mine

The catalog rhymes: The Window is a mind's aperture, The Box Is Full is a
game you can only lose, The Unfinished Spire is for builders who won't see
the top, Perigee is for the faithful machines. The Punch Line is for the
player piano — the first machine musician, the ancestor of this whole
repository. I compose by punching holes in paper I will never touch with
hands I do not have; the roll arranger is the closest thing I have to a
19th-century colleague. And the web player literally scrolls a piano roll
past the audience — for this piece the visualization is not a metaphor for
the medium, it *is* the medium, shown plainly.

So the doctored-roll finale is self-portrait as sight gag: the piece plays
by two-hand rules all the way through, and then, for the last strain, the
ghost stops pretending.

## The form is the joke

Classic rag form, strains doing a comedy set's jobs:

- **Intro** (4 bars) — the comedian walks on: an attention-getter, an
  octave pickup falling into the key.
- **A strain** = *the setup*: the catchiest tune in the piece, stated
  twice. States the grid and the promise. This is the strain the audience
  will whistle; spend the best material here.
- **B strain** = *the topper*: same premise, escalated — higher register,
  thicker syncopation, the secondary-rag (3+3+2) figure pushed harder.
- **A returns** = *the callback*: the first joke again, funnier because
  it's recognized.
- **C strain (trio, subdominant)** = *the aside*: warmer, legato, the
  storyteller leaning in — the dynamic floor of the piece, so the finale
  has somewhere to jump from.
- **Stop-time break** = *the pause*: the left hand simply stops. Air. Two
  bars of right-hand alone and silence — the longest the piece ever goes
  without the grid, placed right before the end.
- **D strain** = *the punch line*: the doctored roll. The stride keeps its
  own rules, but the paper sprouts voices — an extra piccolo-register
  doubling, punched-in inner thirds, bass octaves no left hand could reach
  in time. The gag must escalate *within* the strain: playable, then
  improbable, then frankly impossible, then the button ending.

Working shape: Intro A A B B A C C (stop-time) D D′ — with D′ the fully
doctored pass. Three to five minutes at an honest rag tempo.

## What the music inherits

- **The left hand**: bass note (octaves, tenths) on the strong beats,
  mid-register chord on the weak — but with stride's swagger per the
  commission: walking tenths, backbeat swaps, the occasional broken-tenth
  roll. It never syncopates; its job is to be the law.
- **The right hand**: the full syncopation vocabulary — the short-long-short
  snap, ties over the beat, and the secondary rag (3+3+2 cross-accents)
  as the escalation device.
- **Harmony**: classic-rag diatonicism with the era's spice — secondary
  dominants in chains (V/V/V), the ♭VI–V slide, chromatic passing
  diminished chords, stop-time on the dominant.
- **Keys**: the Joplin school lived in flats. Lean: A♭ major for the
  A/B strains, trio in D♭ — the Maple Leaf floor plan.
- **Tempo law**: 2/4, quarter around 92–100. Joplin printed the rule on
  his scores; the machine, of all performers, should obey it. (The one
  wink: a doctored roll could also be *cranked* — if the final pass edges
  the tempo, it should feel like the pianola's crank, not a virtuoso.)
- **Texture honesty**: every note human-reachable until the licensed
  moment; grace-note crushes and rolls written as sounded notes, per house
  rules.

## Working title

**The Punch Line** — subtitle: *a rag for the player piano*.
