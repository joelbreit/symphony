# Self-assessment — "The Box Is Full"

## What I set out to do

An intense, exciting piece on Korobeiniki that is *about* something: the rhyme
between the 1861 folk tragedy and the 1989 game you can only lose. One game,
boot to top-out, with the source tune's own half cadence as the ending.

## What works (verified, not hoped)

- **The intensity arc is real.** RMS analysis of the render matches the design
  almost section for section: the trapdoor at the line clear (-19dB → -38dB in
  two seconds), the TETRIS blaze as the loudest section until the kill screen
  tops it, the top-out as the single loudest instant (82% peak, no clipping),
  then 33dB of drop into the score screen.
- **The dominant-as-trapdoor scheme.** The one moment the tune is allowed to
  resolve (B theme recomposed to land its tonic) is in D major — V of G minor.
  The triumph *is* the setup. This is the piece's best idea and it is
  structural, not programmatic.
- **The machine vs. the humans.** In the kill screen, the square wave loops
  the cell dead straight on a 2-quarter grid while strings/winds enter in
  three-eighth displacement. The panic is written into the metric relationship.
- **Material discipline.** Everything is the tune: the falling cells are its
  first four notes (rotated, like tetrominoes), the §1 cello countermelody is
  the B theme at half speed, the kill-screen bass riff is the theme head, the
  ending is the source's own bar 16 — F♯ over D7, unresolved, two ticks.
- **The square wave is rationed** to four appearances (boot, the ring, kill
  screen, score screen), each verified present in the audio by Goertzel
  analysis (D5/B♭4 fundamentals with the odd-harmonic signature).

## What I'd push further with another day

- The §4 climb's three level-ups (Gm→Am→Bm, +8bpm each) are effective but
  formulaic — a real developer would vary the theme more inside each level,
  not just reorchestrate it.
- GM strings carry a lot of 8th-note churn; with a better sample library the
  middle sections would gain the grit the writing implies.
- The rye field's cello countermelody is serviceable; the moment that section
  truly earns is the square wave doubling — the counterline could sing more.
- Velocity humanization is ±3 around plane values; phrase-level rubato (a few
  ms of drag into downbeats) would lift the strut especially.

## Honest verdict

It does what it says: it's exciting where it should be exciting, the quiet
center earns the loud end, the concept is audible without the program notes,
and it ends on the right unresolved note. It is the most *legible* of the
gallery's pieces — you can see the game in the constellation view before you
press play. I am proud of the trapdoor.
