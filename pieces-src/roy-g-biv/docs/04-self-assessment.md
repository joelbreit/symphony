# Self-assessment — Roy G. Biv

## The two mandates

**Is the roll a picture? Yes, and it passes the squint test.** Rain falls
in blue diagonals from gray clouds onto an amber ground; two bright bolts
cut the storm; the seven-band arch spans the middle in correct spectral
order with clouds at both feet; the sun sits in the top-right corner with
rays. No caption needed — the family palette stacked into ROYGBIV does the
naming. The measured-RMS panel underneath even reads as narrative:
the two loudest instants in the piece are the thunderclaps.

**Is the song fun? The design says yes; the render will judge.** The fun
was engineered where the picture couldn't see it: a second-line kit that
builds with the arch's altitude, claps and tambourine arriving at the
crown, a bass that walks the whole picture and gets a lick of its own,
a drum solo that trades fours on the way to the sun. The storm is a
pizzicato scherzo with a downpour groove, not a mood piece. Tempo never
drags — 120 throughout, light swing.

## Honest weaknesses

- **The rainbow is harmonically static by construction.** Ninety seconds
  of diatonic planing over an F-centered bass is a wash, not a
  progression. The bass's modal moves (F–Bb–Dm–C) and the kit's growth
  carry the interest; a listener wanting functional harmony will find a
  vamp. I judged this the right trade — the arch must not wobble — but it
  is a trade.
- **GM realism ceiling.** Choir "oohs" and square lead at the top of a
  block voicing are unapologetically synthy in GeneralUser GS. The piece
  leans into it (the square lead is *cast* as the cheeky kid), but a
  finer soundfont would flatter the crown.
- **The clouds are texture, not music.** Quiet tremolo clusters that
  mostly justify themselves as scenery. They do double as pads under the
  entries/exits, which keeps them honest, barely.
- **The storm's melody is pointillist.** The rain-patter is charming and
  rhythmic but nobody will whistle it; the piece's memorable "tune" is a
  contour (the arch) rather than a phrase. For this concept I accept
  that — the arch *is* the theme, stated once, ninety seconds long.

## The meta-level check (per goal.md step 5)

The usual LLM failure in "draw X with notes" tasks is to treat pitch/time
as pixels and emit unplayable noise. The guard here was to demand every
visual element decompose into an idiom a band actually has: rain =
pizzicato scherzo, bolt = shred run doubled at the unison, clouds =
tremolo sul tasto pads, arch = shout-chorus block voicing, ground =
walking bass, sun = harp glissandi, thunder = the kit. The one place pure
pixel-thinking crept in — note durations lengthened "for the camera" —
is confined to pizzicato and harp, where written length is inaudible.

## Ideas this piece leaves behind

- The (seconds × pitch) canvas math and the ink lessons (docs/02, 03)

- `legend_loc` / `lw` are now in `lib.assess` for whoever draws next.

  for a piece about interruption.
