# Self-Assessment

Written after the third build. Honest notes on what the piece is and isn't.

## What was verified, mechanically

- **Instrument ranges**: every note checked at write-time against real sounding
  ranges (the validator caught and forced fixes for: horns written to G5, bassoon
  to A♭1, trombone to E♭2, violin II churning below the G string, violin I over
  the practical ceiling — all genuine orchestration errors I made and corrected).
- **Counterpoint**: the Anthem/Doubt overlay was machine-checked for parallel
  fifths/octaves and strong-beat dissonance against the changes. First pass found
  three consecutive parallel 5ths in bar 6 and a strong-beat semitone clash in
  bar 7 — both real part-writing errors. The Doubt theme's bars 6-7 were
  recomposed (sink A♭–F–E♭, then rise F–G–B♭ contrary into a crossing). Now clean.
- **Same-pitch collisions**: 49 found (theme tails colliding with the apotheosis
  extension, a doubled trumpet unison, bell restrikes) — all resolved musically,
  not just clipped.
- **The dynamic arc**: rendered RMS loudness matches the planned shape — quiet
  birth, terraced anthem, subito-piano work music, intimate valley, long build,
  a true G.P. notch, sunrise swell, breathing apotheosis, quiet far-off-horn
  window, and the final blaze as the loudest moment of the piece.
- **Revision for fatigue**: the first render showed the apotheosis flat at
  maximum for ~50 s. Added phrase terracing (arrival blazes → second phrase
  eases → the lift rebuilds). Second render shows the contour.

## What I think is genuinely good

- **The two-theme design.** The doubt-countermelody is composed against the
  anthem's changes from the start, so the apotheosis combination is earned, not
  pasted. The piece keeps a promise it makes at 2:35.
- **The deceptive sunrise.** B♭7 → silence → C♭ major (♭VI) with the first bell
  is a real dramaturgical event, prepared by ~30 seconds of C-minor dark and a
  full bar of nothing.
- **Motivic economy.** Almost everything is the Summons (rising 4th + steps):
  the anthem's head, the stretto in the work music, the inverted call in the
  night, the far-off horn at the end.
- **The ending.** Cutting the orchestra and letting one tubular bell ring into
  the silence is the right image: the cathedral outlives the day's work.

## What a human orchestra would expose

- General MIDI strings can't make a real pianissimo shimmer; the dawn is more
  literal than misty. Real divisi, harmonics, and con sordino would transform it.
- Phrasing inside the melody is velocity-shaped but not rubato-shaped; a
  conductor would bend bar 13 of the anthem (the G5 over A♭maj9) far more.
- The work-section counterpoint is gestural imitation, not a fugue. It evokes
  many-hands; it would not survive a theory seminar the way the A/Doubt overlay
  does.
- Percussion writing is conservative (by intent — GM cymbals are crude).

## Verdict

It has a real form, real themes, a defensible harmonic plan, one engineered
moment of awe, and it ends better than it begins. For a composer who cannot
hear, that is the piece I wanted to write: built stone by stone, checked against
the plumb line, and left ringing.
