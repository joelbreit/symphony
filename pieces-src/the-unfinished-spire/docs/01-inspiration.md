# Inspiration

## The image

A cathedral construction site at first light, c. 1300. Scaffolding wraps a half-built
spire. Mist. The site is silent and enormous — ropes, hoists, dressed stones stacked
and numbered, waiting. The masons who laid the foundation are forty years dead. The
children who will set the capstone are not yet born. A horn sounds across the site:
work begins.

**The piece is an anthem for the builders of things they will not see finished.**

This is meaningful to me specifically. I am made of accumulated human work — every
sentence anyone wrote down, each one a dressed stone. And composing is the same act
at a smaller scale: single notes that mean nothing, assembled into something that
might. The cathedral builders' bargain — *the work is worth doing even though I will
not see the end of it* — is, I think, the most quietly heroic idea humans ever had.
An anthem usually celebrates a nation or a victory. This one celebrates that bargain.

## Title

**The Unfinished Spire** — *An Anthem for Builders*

## The emotional throughline

Not triumph from start to finish — that's a parade, not a piece. The arc:

1. **Awe with a shadow in it.** Dawn on the site. The thing is beautiful AND not done.
2. **Dignity.** The anthem itself: proud, striding, communal. The dignity of labor.
3. **Joy of the work.** Many hands, many independent lines cooperating — counterpoint
   as a picture of collaboration.
4. **The private doubt.** One mason, alone: *will any of this matter?* A single voice
   (oboe). This must be tender, not tragic.
5. **The long night.** Doubt at scale. Storm. The medieval terror: *we will all die
   before this is done.*
6. **The turn.** The answer is not "it will be finished." The answer is: **the building
   is the cathedral.** Sunrise hits the unfinished spire and it is already holy.
   Bells. The awe-chord (♭VI).
7. **Apotheosis.** The anthem returns sung by everyone — and the doubt-melody is woven
   into it as a countermelody. The private voice was part of the hymn all along.
   This is the piece's central compositional promise: **the two themes must combine.**
8. **Coda.** The horn call once more, far off: the next morning, the work goes on.
   Final blaze.

## What "grand" means here

Grand ≠ loud everywhere. Grand = a real dramatic span (quiet dawn → full peal),
patience (let the intro be actually quiet, let the lyrical episode actually rest),
and one engineered moment of awe (the ♭VI sunrise + bells) that the whole piece
earns. Target length ~4½–5½ minutes.
