# Blueprint — "Majority Rules"

## Ground rules

- **Band:** cornet, clarinet, alto sax, trombone, sousaphone, snare, bass drum
  and mounted cymbal — exactly the *Cut Loose* roster.
- **Home:** B-flat major. E-flat for the amendment/opposition; G minor colors
  the filibuster; dominant chains always discharge back into B-flat.
- **Tempo:** 176 bpm, light swing (0.58), pushing to 184 for the vote.
- **Length:** 164 bars, about 3:45 plus the last chord.
- **Texture law:** one lead talks in eighths; the others either sustain, riff
  in its holes, or interrupt as a coordinated event.

## Materials

1. **THE MOTION:** D–F–B-flat up, then A–G–F–D down. A two-bar public call
   expanded into a sixteen-bar strain. It begins after an eighth rest, as if
   the chair had to bang the table before speaking.
2. **THE AMENDMENT:** G–B-flat–E-flat, smoother and more legato, with a
   chromatic E-flat-minor shadow before it agrees to come home. Alto sax owns
   it first; trombone later makes it impolite.
3. **AYE:** the motion's first three pitches compressed into two beats. Each
   player answers the same roll-call question differently.
4. **THE GAVEL:** three band hits — long, short, short — used to call the room
   to order and later abused in the false endings.
5. **THE COUNT:** a two-beat sousaphone line with a walk every four bars. At
   the roll call it changes to four-to-the-bar; by the unanimous vote it is
   the force carrying the whole room outside.

## Form

| Bars | Section | Event |
|---:|---|---|
| 1–4 | Call to order | Snare press roll; gavel hits; sousaphone states the count |
| 5–20 | The motion | Cornet presents the main strain; clarinet comments, bone connects |
| 21–36 | The amendment | Alto proposes the E-flat strain; cornet objects in the gaps |
| 37–52 | Motion, amended | Clarinet takes the motion while alto sings the amendment underneath |
| 53–60 | Point of order | Stop-time one-bar objections passed around the four horns |
| 61–76 | Honorable opposition | Trombone takes the amendment, tailgate-first, in E-flat |
| 77–84 | Rebuttal | Cornet and bone trade twos over a dominant chain back home |
| 85–100 | The motion again | Full collective chorus; first unequivocal B-flat arrival |
| 101–116 | Filibuster | Clarinet solo over G minor/circle changes grows from agile to absurd |
| 117–124 | Table the motion | Horns drop out; sousaphone and the two drummers conduct business |
| 125–140 | Roll call | Cornet, clarinet, alto, trombone answer four bars each |
| 141–156 | Unanimous | Motion in block harmony, amendment as counterline, four-beat bass; push |
| 157–160 | Recount | Three false endings, each contradicted by a drummer or horn |
| 161–164 | Majority rules | Unison hook, one final vote, fat B-flat |

## Craft gates

- The motion and amendment must each be whistleable and rhythmically distinct.
- All four horn personalities must be audible without program notes.
- Motion + amendment at bar 141 must interlock rather than merely stack.
- There must be two real dynamic valleys (call to order, table the motion).
- The three false endings must be different jokes, not three identical tags.
- The final B-flat should feel elected.
