# Inspiration — "Majority Rules"

## The image

A brass band has wandered into a neighborhood town meeting. Nobody throws
them out quickly enough.

The cornet appoints himself chair and pounds out a four-note motion. The
clarinet heckles from the back row. The alto sax is the only person who has
actually read the agenda and offers a graceful amendment. The trombone rises
for the honorable opposition, sounding neither honorable nor opposed to
having a good time. The sousaphone is the clerk: it counts every vote in
root-and-fifth half notes and quietly drags every procedural detour back to
B-flat. The snare writes minutes no court reporter could read; the bass drum
is the public gallery stamping its feet.

The meeting becomes a parade without ever adjourning.

## Why this is a different piece

*High Street Riot* is the lifecycle of one vamp: invented, accumulated,
fought over, detonated. *Cut Loose* is one tune living first as a dirge and
then as a second line. This piece is a **rondo of incompatible speeches**.
Its pleasure comes from interruption and eventual agreement:

- the **motion** is a square, instantly singable sixteen-bar strain in B-flat;
- the **amendment** is a smoother strain leaning toward E-flat;
- the **objections** are stop-time one-bar interjections;
- the **filibuster** lets the clarinet refuse to surrender the floor;
- the **roll call** gives every horn the same two-bar question and a personal
  answer;
- the **unanimous vote** puts motion and amendment together in block harmony.

The climax is not louder repetition. It is the first moment when everybody
is obnoxious in the same direction.

## The people's key

B-flat major is physically native to the cornet, clarinet, trombone, and
street-band tradition even though the score is kept at concert pitch. Its
open, public sound is the premise. E-flat is the amendment (IV, the room next
door); G minor is where the filibuster briefly darkens; dominant chains are
procedural maneuvers. Every road returns to B-flat because the sousaphone has
already counted the votes.
