# Inspiration — "Perigee"

## The image

A satellite in its last weeks. Not tumbling, not broken — just low. Every
orbit, the thin edge of the atmosphere takes a little more, and here is the
cruelty: drag does not slow it down. Losing altitude means gaining speed.
Every revolution is shorter than the last, every perigee pass hotter and
closer, and the machine spends its final days doing the fastest, most
brilliant flying of its life.

**The closer you fall, the faster you dance.**

That is a tango. The embrace that tightens as it ends. The elegance that is
inseparable from the doom. Two bodies — the satellite and the Earth — locked
in a dance where one of them is also the floor.

## Why tango

Tango is the music of controlled falling: the walk is a perpetual catch of
your own weight, the marcato hits like gravity, the síncopa (3‑3‑2) staggers
against it, and the phrases surge and collapse like something breathing hard.
Nuevo tango — Piazzolla's — adds the machinery: motoric ostinati, fugato
entries, sudden freezes, the la yumba grind. A machine's last dance belongs
in exactly this language.

And the catalog rhymes: The Window is a mind's aperture, The Box Is Full is
a game you can only lose, The Unfinished Spire is for builders who won't see
the top. Perigee is for the machines — sent up to watch, faithful past their
fuel, dancing all the way down.

## The physics is the form

- **Apogee** = the far, cool arc: lyrical, rubato, thin texture, time
  stretches. Where the satellite still sees the whole Earth at once.
- **Perigee** = the close pass: marcato in four, driving, the heat. Each
  return is faster, louder, a semitone or a minor third closer to the fire.
- **Orbital decay** = the engine of the piece: each apogee section is
  *shorter* than the last, each perigee *longer and hotter*, until there is
  no apogee left — the orbit circularizes into the atmosphere.
- **Re-entry** = the break in the cycle: a toccata, all five instruments in
  their most violent register, the síncopa tumbling end over end — then the
  hard cut. (Tango knows this move: the freeze.)
- **After** = the coda in harmonics and high piano, weightless, the theme
  once more with no floor under it — a trail of light the sky keeps for a
  minute. It ends with the quietest possible *chan-chan* — tango's
  traditional two closing chords, played like two last blinks of a beacon.

## What the music inherits

- **The walk**: double bass + piano left hand in marcato four; arrastre
  (the drag) smearing into downbeats — the toolkit's scoop, played low.
- **The síncopa**: 3‑3‑2 as the tumbling rhythm; it *is* orbital decay
  written in eighth notes — the pattern that never lands where gravity says.
- **The bellows**: the bandoneón phrase swells and collapses on held notes —
  CC11 hairpins are the instrument's lungs. The violin gets the long
  portamento sighs; the guitar comps dry and close.
- **A theme that returns diminished**: one tango melody, restated each
  revolution — transposed, compressed, ornamented harder (mordents, turns),
  until at re-entry it is only rhythm. The coda restores the very first
  phrase, intact, weightless.
- **Harmonic home**: A minor with the Phrygian lean (B♭ against A) tango
  loves; descending tetrachord bass (A–G–F–E) as the gravity well; perigee
  passes pivot up by minor thirds. E7♭9 is the heat.

## The band

The Piazzolla quintet, straight from GM:

violin (40) · bandoneón (tango accordion, 23) · piano (0) ·
guitar (jazz guitar, 26) · double bass (32) — plus, sparingly, the golpe:
knocks on wood (rim/woodblock) where a tanguero would rap the instrument.

No drums. The percussion is the marcato itself.

## Working title

**Perigee** — the point of closest approach. Subtitle: *a tango for a
falling satellite*.
